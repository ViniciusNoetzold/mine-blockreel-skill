## Method and limits (read before trusting the numbers)

- Frames at 1/s (370 stills, viewed as labelled contact sheets), scene-change detection with ffmpeg
  (`gt(scene, t)`), `cropdetect`, `blackdetect`, `silencedetect` and a per-5 s RMS loudness curve.
- **I did not listen to the audio**, so music genre, sound effects and the spoken words are unknown.
  Only the loudness envelope (silences, quiet/loud passages) is reported. Dialogue is *inferred* from
  mouth-shape changes on the characters.
- 1 fps sampling under-samples fast action; the cut counts below are bounds, not exact.
- Subtitles: none in the body of the film; the only on-screen text is a closing thank-you card.

## Rhythm and structure

1. **0–10 s, hook:** starts in a wide on characters walking in a desolate field, then within 3 s a
   huge foreground character fills the frame (scale shock) before returning to wide. Motion and scale
   change in the first 5 s, no title card.
2. **10–25 s, world-building:** a long low-angle tilt up a monolithic structure against a cloudy sky.
   Slow, big, no characters: establishes place and scale.
3. **25–60 s, setup + dread:** three characters in a medium shot, then a *reveal by scale* (a few
   zombies in the foreground, then pull back until hundreds fill the horizon). Followed by reaction close-ups.
4. **60–100 s, siege + sacrifice:** crowd chaos, one oversized monster, a glowing altar, sparks, a white flash, a
   fade to black (the first act break).
5. **103–205 s, duel:** new location with a different color world (violet void / pale ground). Dense cutting,
   extreme close-ups, whip pans, slow-motion, energy-effect weapons, a charge-up flash.
6. **205–255 s, escalation:** the stakes grow from character scale to creature scale (two large flying
   mobs, riders on their backs). Wide aerial shots alternate with tight impact shots.
7. **255–282 s, resolution:** quiet beat (a character crawling, red cube particles), final blow, a very long
   white-out. This is the emotional peak, and it is held *without* new action.
8. **282–291 s:** montage of earlier-series imagery, partly in game-HUD view, dissolving through white.
9. **292–310 s:** 18 s of black. Audio is at music level here, content unknown to me.
10. **310–354 s:** an epilogue on one nearly static, slowly drifting shot of a burning scene (smoke and
    fire particles), with a late character entry from the top of frame. Then a plain end card
    (~15 s, centered text on dark purple).

Principle: **energy is a shape, not a constant.** Quiet → loud → one deliberate silence + white → long
quiet tail. The film earns its loud middle by opening and closing quiet.

## Shot types and camera

- **Wide establishing** with slow movement, used once per location.
- **Scale reveals:** foreground object → pull back to the full crowd. Cheap and very effective in a block world.
- **Extreme close-ups** on faces and weapons during the duel, often with a head-sized object filling half the frame.
- **Low and Dutch angles** (tilted horizon) during action; level horizon during calm.
- **Whip pans / smear frames** as transitions *inside* a scene: the camera sweeps past something and the next
  shot begins mid-sweep.
- **Handheld shake** layered on almost every action shot, not just on impacts.
- **Foreground blur / depth of field:** close objects soft, subject sharp. Strong **motion blur** on fast limbs.
- **Slow-motion** on arrows and key hits, then snap back to speed.
- **Camera cut on a flash**: a bright frame covers the cut.
- **Long locked-off epilogue shot** with only particles moving: the opposite of the middle.

| Duel arena | violet star-field sky, pale cream ground, dark purple pillars | cool/warm split: character lit cool, ground bright |
| Epilogue | warm red-magenta haze, orange fire, grey smoke | only warm palette of the film |

- **Glowing eyes** (pure white with a horizontal streak) are the signature highlight: they pull the eye to the face in dark frames.
- **Bloom** on emissive things (energy, fire, eyes, crystals) and **full-frame white flashes** as transitions.
- **Color shifts mark act breaks**: navy → violet → warm red. The viewer feels the chapter change before any caption.
- Volumetric-looking glow beams from a source; low-contrast distance haze.

## How characters act

- Rigid blocky bodies, but **faces swap**: eyes widen, mouth changes between at least 3 shapes
  (closed, small "o", wide open/shout). Wounds appear as red patches on the face. This face-swapping is the
  single biggest difference from a plain-box rig.
- Strong **anticipation**: crouch before a lunge, wind-up before a swing, head snap back on a hit.
- **Overshoot and follow-through**: weapons and capes lag behind the body, limbs overshoot then settle.
- **Exaggerated poses** held for a few frames on key hits ("impact frame"), with the rest on shorter timing.
- Mostly low, wide fighting stances; characters often lean *into* the camera.
- Reaction shots: a character looks off-screen with a held stare, then the cut reveals what they see.
- Many characters share a few shared motion vocabularies (run, roll, fall, crawl, hit-stagger, swing).

## Dialogue and text

- Dialogue appears to be voiced (mouth shapes change in the quiet pocket ≈ 50–70 s) and **no on-screen subtitles
  are used**; the film relies on performance and sound.
- Text is used only for the end card: one sentence, centered, bold condensed type, high contrast, held ~15 s.
- Character name tags appear in the montage as in-game floating labels (a nostalgia cue, not a subtitle).

## Sound (loudness only; unheard)

- Loud and continuous in the action, which suggests a driving music bed (not confirmed).
- A **quiet pocket** at the setup/dialogue, and **a hard silence for ~4 s right after the final white-out**. The silence + white is
  the emotional beat.
- 18 s of black with audible sound at music level.
- Epilogue is quiet (−26 to −34 dB) under a static shot.

## Special effects

- **Particles:** gold sparks around a light source, falling dust/debris from a tower, gray smoke cubes drifting upward, orange fire
  tiles, red cubes for blood, pink/white pixel shards in explosions. All are *cubes or square sprites*, never round blobs.
- **Explosions** = a bright core flash covering 1–3 frames, then expanding pixel debris, then smoke.
- **Energy weapons:** blue lightning-like ribbons and a solid red blade-glow. Ribbons are drawn with a thick glowing
  core plus soft halo.
- **Crowds:** several hundred instances of one mob, staggered animation phase so they don't move in sync.
- **Creatures:** custom multi-part block models with their own rigs (wings, three heads, tail), purple glowing eyes/mouth.
- **Screen-space:** letterbox, vignette, bloom, motion blur, slight chromatic/lens streaks on bright eyes.

## What makes it nostalgic

- **The 2012–2015 fan-animation grammar:** soft cinematic lighting on cube skins, bloom on emissives, glowing white
  eyes on the villain, a violet star-field arena, pixel-cube particles.
- **Familiar archetypes:** a skin-pack hero vs. a legendary villain, mobs as an army, the End dimension as the final stage.
- **Cast montage and a thank-you card** → the "end of a season" ritual familiar from YouTube series.
- **Texture crispness:** nearest-neighbour textures with soft lighting; the blocks stay pixelated while the lighting is cinematic.

## Principles to carry into BlockReel (no scene is copied)

1. Open with motion and a scale surprise within 3 s.
2. Shape energy: quiet start → dense middle → one deliberate silence/white-out → quiet tail.
3. One palette per location; shift palette at act breaks.
4. Reveal scale by pulling back from a foreground object.
5. Use whip-pan or flash transitions *inside* action scenes; save plain cuts for dialogue.
6. Put a light-source highlight (glowing eyes, fire, crystal) where the viewer must look.
7. Handheld shake on all action shots; static camera only for calm moments.
8. Add face states (eyes/mouth) before adding more body clips.
9. Anticipation + overshoot + follow-through make rigid bodies feel alive.
10. Particles are cubes; always seeded so the render is deterministic.
11. Hold the emotional beat; do not fill it with action.
12. End on a quiet, wide or static shot, then a plain text card.
